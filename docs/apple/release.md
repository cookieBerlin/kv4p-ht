# App Store release

How the app goes from TestFlight into the App Store: milestone 99.1 of the [roadmap](04-roadmap.md). The first
release is version 1.0.0 ([ADR-0070](adr/0070-own-app-version.md)), for iPhone, iPad and Mac at the same time, in one
app record ([ADR-0035](adr/0035-testflight.md)). Until then the app reaches testers through TestFlight only
([ADR-0036](adr/0036-no-distribution-outside-app-store.md)).

**Status:** part A is prepared ([ADR-0076](adr/0076-screenshots.md)); part B is done at the release.

## Decisions

- **Platforms:** iPhone, iPad and Mac in the first release.
- **Language:** English only, in the store as on the website.
- **Screenshots:** raw, without device frames or captions. Light and dark mixed in the store. No App Preview video.
- **One set of images** for the store, the website and the [manual](manual.md), generated by a script, so that they
  can be made again after every change of the user interface.
- **Source:** the current `main`, not an older TestFlight build. At the release, the images are generated again from
  the release build, as the store wants images of the app that is submitted.

## Part A: prepared ahead of the release

### A1. `make screenshots`

- One UI test in the demo mode (`-uitest-demo`) goes through the screens and saves an image of each.
- A second argument of the demo mode, such as `-uitest-appearance dark`, sets the colour scheme of the app. The test
  goes through the screens in light, starts the app again in dark and goes through them once more. The app is built
  once and the simulator started once, for both.
- One simulator at a time, shut down after its run. The status bar shows 9:41, a full battery and full signal
  (`simctl status_bar override`).
- The Mac: the app window only, taken by the same test with the same argument.
- **Output:** the images in full size for App Store Connect go to `.build/screenshots/` and stay out of the
  repository. Smaller, compressed copies (about 500 pixels wide) for the website and the manual are committed.
- Images are generated on purpose only, not in CI, so that the repository does not grow with every run.
- The sizes App Store Connect asks for (November 2026): iPhone with Dynamic Island, medium display, 1206 × 2622
  (iPhone 18 Pro simulator); iPad 13-inch, 2064 × 2752 (iPad Pro 13-inch (M5) simulator); Mac 2560 × 1600. They are
  checked there again at the release. iPhone Duo screenshots become required in April 2027 for apps built with the
  iOS 27.1 SDK; they are taken by hand-folding the simulator ([ADR-0076](adr/0076-screenshots.md)).
- [ADR-0076](adr/0076-screenshots.md) records the workflow.

### A2. Motifs

The store shows up to ten images per platform; the first three are seen in the search results. The order is set in
`scripts/screenshots.sh`; light and dark take turns.

| # | iPhone | Also in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The main screen: frequency, S-meter, memories, **Push to talk** | website, manual |
| 2 | A chat over APRS | website, manual |
| 3 | The APRS map with stations and repeaters | website, manual |
| 4 | The APRS packets | website, manual |
| 5 | A scan through the memories | website, manual |
| 6 | Settings, *Memories*: groups and the imports of RepeaterBook and DARC lists | website, manual |
| 7 | Settings, *Simulator*: trying the app without a radio | website, manual |
| 8 | Settings, *Radio*: FreeDV 2400B | website, manual |

- **iPad:** memories and APRS side by side, the chat, the map, the scan, and the settings pages.
- **Mac:** the same, and on its *Radio* page the firmware update over USB, which only the Mac has.
- **Manual only:** the settings with the connection on top.
- **Demo content:** placeholder callsigns only (`N0CALL` to `N7CALL`), fictional repeaters, and a chat in the
  abbreviations of radio amateurs; the stations stand apart from each other on the map.
- The import itself is not shown: it needs a file chosen in the system's dialog, which a UI test cannot reach.

### A3. Store texts

In [app-store-listing.md](app-store-listing.md), next to the [App Review notes](app-review-notes.md), with the limit
of each field:

- **Name:** *kv4p HT (by DB3AC)* (30 characters).
- **Subtitle** (30 characters), such as *Control your kv4p HT radio*.
- **Promotional text** (170 characters; can be changed without a review).
- **Description** (up to 4000 characters), from the features on the website. It says that the app is an unofficial
  port, not affiliated with the kv4p HT project, and that it needs the radio.
- **Keywords** (100 characters), such as ham radio, amateur radio, APRS, FreeDV, VHF, UHF, transceiver, repeater.
- **Category** (proposed: Utilities), age rating, copyright line.
- **Support, marketing and privacy policy URLs:** the pages of the website.

### A4. Website and manual

- **Gallery:** the home page shows iPhone, iPad and Mac at the top, and below them a gallery of the motifs above.
- **Light and dark:** a switch for *System*, *Light* and *Dark*, with a few lines of JavaScript; the choice is kept in
  the browser (`localStorage`). Without JavaScript, the page follows the system. No cookies, no tracking.
- **Images in both schemes:** each image exists in light and dark; CSS classes show the one that fits the chosen
  scheme, so that the images follow the switch and not only the system.
- **Manual:** one image per main section, in both schemes on the website. On GitHub the manual shows the light image.
  The image paths must work on GitHub and on the website ([`links.lua`](site/links.lua)).
- Every image has an alternative text; the manual stays complete without the images.

## Part B: at the release

1. Set `MARKETING_VERSION` to 1.0.0 ([ADR-0070](adr/0070-own-app-version.md)) and give the changelog its section.
2. Generate the images again with `make screenshots` from the release build, and commit the website copies.
3. Upload the build with `make testflight` ([ADR-0035](adr/0035-testflight.md)) for iOS and macOS.
4. In App Store Connect, for each platform: the images from `.build/screenshots/`, the texts from
   `app-store-listing.md`, the [App Review notes](app-review-notes.md), the privacy details, then submit for review.
5. After the approval: on the website, the App Store badge (with Apple's artwork and under its guidelines) instead of
   the TestFlight steps; TestFlight stays as a note for beta testers. Mark 99.1 as done in the roadmap.
