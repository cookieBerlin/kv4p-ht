# 04 — Roadmap

Status: draft. Each milestone is developed test-first, committed step by step and
merged via pull request through the CI gate ([ADR-0013](adr/0013-ci-pr-gate.md)).
Every milestone that changes user-visible behaviour updates the
[manual](manual.md) and [FAQ](faq.md).

**Milestones:** ⬜ open · ✅ implemented and merged. A milestone's pull request ticks its
box. Hardware test results are in [hardware-tests.md](hardware-tests.md).

## Phase 5 — Repeater lists

|   | # | Milestone | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ | 5.1 | **DARC repeater list import** | A memory gains a kind, *repeater* or *frequency*, and three optional fields: a comment, a latitude and a longitude; existing memories migrate and keep syncing (3.9), and one with an offset becomes a repeater, any other a frequency. The memory editor edits them, the list of memories marks a repeater with a symbol and shows the comment below the frequency where there is room, not with the largest text sizes. The import of memories reads the CSV export of the DARC repeater lists (relaislisten.darc.de) as well as RepeaterBook's and tells them apart by their header; the file is chosen by the user, as the lists have no licence for a download by the app. Columns: *Call* becomes the name, *QRG* the receive frequency, *Input*, the repeater's receive frequency, gives the offset (empty: simplex, offset 0), *CTCSS* the tone, *Info* the comment, *Breite* and *Länge* the position, taken as they are. Imported memories are repeaters. Only FM repeaters are imported; an entry without a mode counts as FM. The preview says how many entries are left out and why: another mode, outside the radio's range, already in the group. The file may be UTF-8 or Windows-1252, with a decimal comma. RepeaterBook's import fills the new fields too where its export has them. Test data uses placeholders only. ADR. |
| ✅ | 5.2a | **Pages that can be switched off** | A settings page *Pages* switches APRS off, on by default and on this device only. Off, the packets, the chats and the APRS settings go, and the app receives and sends no APRS: no beacons, messages, acknowledgements, digipeating or APRS-IS. The other APRS settings stay for when it is on again ([ADR-0066](adr/0066-hideable-pages.md)). |
| ✅ | 5.2b | **Map** | A page *Map* after the chats, with MapKit, shows the user's position and, as layers to switch on and off, the repeaters, the frequencies (off by default) and the beacons that have a position. A choice of band shows the radio's band (by default), 2 m, 70 cm or all, and the markers show the band. A tap on a memory shows it and tunes to it if the radio receives it. The map does not follow the position; the system's button centres it. A switch shows the same points as a list by distance, for VoiceOver. *Pages* gains a switch for the map ([ADR-0068](adr/0068-map.md)). |
| ✅ | 5.2c | **APRS stations on the map** | A layer shows the APRS stations and objects heard, each at its last position only, within the last 15 minutes, hour (by default), 6 hours, 24 hours or all, from the history as well. A tap shows the station and leads to its packet or chat. Without APRS, the layer goes. |
| ⬜ | 5.5 | **RepeaterBook download** | The options are evaluated first: embedded web page as in the Android app, official API, stored login. The evaluation covers RepeaterBook's terms. ## Phase 6 — SSTV

|   | # | Milestone | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⬜ | 6.1 | **SSTV receive** | The milestone starts with two decisions: which modes the app supports, and whether an existing library can be reused or the decoder is written from scratch (including its licence). The app then decodes SSTV images from the received audio and shows and saves them. The decoder is tested with generated test signals, as the Android app has no SSTV to serve as an oracle. ADR. |
| ⬜ | 6.2 | **SSTV transmit** | The user chooses an image, and the app sends it as it is in the chosen modes; the callsign is part of the image. The app's own TX time limit does not apply to it, and the transmission can be stopped at any time. Listen-only mode blocks it, and the frequency limits stay in force. ## Phase 7 — Simulators on the network

|   | # | Milestone | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⬜ | 7.1 | **Simulators that hear each other** | Simulated radios of the app on several Macs, iPhones and iPads in the same local network find each other through Bonjour and connect directly, with the local network permission only; no multicast entitlement is needed. Together they form one simulated channel, on which everything the app does over a real radio works: what one transmits, the others hear when tuned to that frequency, with offset, tones and squelch as on air. That covers FM voice, FreeDV 2400B, the repeater access tone, APRS packets with positions, objects, weather and messages, acknowledgements, chats, beacons, digipeating and the S-meter. Only one station transmits at a time per frequency, and a second one is heard as on air. A session holds only the devices that joined it on purpose, and nothing leaves the local network. The simulator alone, without a network, keeps working as before, and the real radio path is not touched. The milestone starts with an ADR on the protocol between the simulators, and may be split into parts, such as APRS first and audio after it. |
