# ADR-0068: Map

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-11-11

## Context

Since milestone 5.1, a memory has a kind (repeater, frequency or beacon) and, optionally, a position, for
example from the DARC repeater lists. Milestone 5.2b puts those memories on a map, with the user's own position.
The Android app has no map, so it is neither a model nor a test oracle here, and its code stays untouched. The APRS
stations follow in 5.2c.

## Decision

- **MapKit, through SwiftUI's `Map`.** It is part of the system on all three platforms and needs no package, no key
  and no fee. Dark mode, the controls for the user's position, the compass and the scale, and VoiceOver for the
  markers come with it.
  - MapLibre with OpenStreetMap tiles would be a new dependency. It would also need a tile server, as the usage
    policy of OpenStreetMap's own servers does not allow an app to use them heavily.
  - Without a network connection, MapKit shows only the tiles it has cached. The FAQ says so.
- **A page of its own, *Map*, after the chats.** On a phone, it is a page of the ring (ADR-0040). In a wide window
  it is a third part of the switch beside the memories, after *Packets* and *Chats*. Without APRS, the map has that
  side to itself.
- **It can be switched off** on the settings page *Pages* (ADR-0066). It is on by default, and the switch stays
  on this device. Off, the page goes, and the app reads no position for it.
- **Layers, one for each kind of memory:** repeaters, frequencies and beacons. Frequencies are off by default, as
  most of them are simplex channels without a fixed place.
  - The layers come from all groups, not only the group the list shows.
  - The colour of a marker shows its kind: repeaters blue, frequencies green, beacons orange. The menu names the
    colours, as it shows none itself.
- **A choice of band:** *Radio's band* (the default), *2 m*, *70 cm* or *All*.
  - A kv4p HT has one band, so a choice fits better than a switch for each. By default, nothing needs setting:
    with a radio connected, the map shows only its band, like the list of memories. Before a radio is known, it
    shows 2 m and 70 cm.
  - The band comes from the module that receives the frequency, as for the radio. Memories that neither module
    receives, such as 6 m, show only with *All*.
  - The mark in a marker shows the band, *2m* or *70*, and a dot for another band. A marker of MapKit holds one
    mark only, and the colour is taken by the kind. The list names the band after the frequency.
- **APRS stations (5.2c):** a fourth layer, on by default, with each station or object at its last position,
  marked with its APRS symbol (ADR-0054).
  - Only packets with a position count: positions, objects and weather. A message has none, so a station that only
    sends messages is not on the map.
  - A time window chooses how far back: 15 minutes, 1 hour (the default), 6 hours, 24 hours or all.
  - The positions come from the packets in memory and from the history (ADR-0057), as memory holds only the newest
    packets. The map reads the history while it is shown and when the window changes, at most 5,000 packets.
  - An object stands for itself, under its name, and goes when its owner removes it. Our own station has no distance.
  - A tap shows its kind, when it was heard, its distance and its comment. *Packets* shows its packets on the
    packets page, found by its callsign, and *Chat* opens the chat with it, but not for an object or our own station.
  - Without APRS, the layer and its stations go.
- **What stays on this device:** the layers, the band, the time window and the choice between map and list, as
  the screens differ.
- **The user's own position:**
  - It is read only while the map is on screen and access is granted. MapKit's dot for it follows it there as the
    system does; for the distances, the app reads it as for the APRS list (ADR-0053): when the map appears, and then
    at most once a minute.
  - The map does not ask for access when it opens. While the question is open, a button asks it. The dot and the
    system's button for the position are there only with access granted, as MapKit asks for it itself otherwise.
  - The map does not follow the position. The system's button centres it.
- **What the map shows first:** all its points and the user's position, with a margin around them, or a few
  kilometres around a single one. It frames them anew when the points change, with the memories, the stations, the
  layers or the band, and once when the user's position arrives; once the user moves the map, it stays there. *Show all*, beside
  the layers, frames them again at any time. With nothing to
  show, the map keeps the system's default.
- **A tap on a marker** shows the memory: its name, frequency, comment and distance. *Tune* is there only while the
  radio is connected and receives the memory, as in the list.
- **A list instead of the map:** a switch shows the same points as a list, the nearest first, with the distance and
  direction. A map is hard to use with VoiceOver; the list is easier, and each of its rows says the kind, the name,
  the frequency, the comment and the distance. A tap on a row tunes to the memory.
- **The logic is in KV4PRadio, without MapKit:** the layers, the points, their order, their distances and the first
  frame. It is tested there like the rest of the model. The view only draws it.

## Consequences

- MapKit loads the tiles of the part of the map on screen from Apple. Apple sees which part of the map the user
  looks at, but not the memories on it. The position stays on the device, as for the APRS list.
- A frame across the date line spans the long way round. That is acceptable for memories within the range of a
  handheld radio.
- On a phone, a swipe on the map moves the map, not the page. The bar below changes the page.
- Many stations make a crowded map; the time window keeps it short, and the layer can go.
